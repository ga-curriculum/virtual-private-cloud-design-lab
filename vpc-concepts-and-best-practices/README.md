## What is a VPC?

Before we get started, let's make sure we understand what a VPC is and why it's important.

A VPC is a virtual network dedicated to your cloud account. For this lab, wee're going to focus only on AWS, but VPCs also exist for other Cloud Service Providers (Azure, GCP, etc).

It's logically isolated from other virtual networks in the AWS Cloud, providing you with complete control over your virtual networking environment.

With a VPC, you can:

- Launch AWS resources, like EC2 instances, into a virtual network that you've defined

- Configure your VPC's IP address range, subnets, route tables, and network gateways

- Control inbound and outbound network traffic using security groups and network access control lists (ACLs)

- Connect your VPC to other VPCs, your on-premises data center, or the internet, as needed

In essence, a VPC is your own slice of the AWS cloud where you have full control over your network configuration.

## Why VPC Design Matters

Designing a VPC may seem straightforward at first glance, but there are many considerations to ensure your cloud network is secure, scalable, and efficient.

A well-designed VPC can:

- Protect your AWS resources from unauthorized access

- Enable you to scale your application seamlessly as demand grows

- Optimize network traffic flow for better performance

- Facilitate compliance with industry regulations and corporate policies

On the flip side, a poorly designed VPC can lead to security vulnerabilities, performance bottlenecks, management headaches, and costly overprovisioning.

That's why it's crucial to follow VPC design best practices from the start.

By architecting your VPC right the first time, you can lay a solid foundation for your cloud deployment that will pay dividends down the road.
