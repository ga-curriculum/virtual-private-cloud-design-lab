## Why diagram?

Now it's time to visualize your design by creating a diagram!

Before we dive in, let's consider why creating a diagram of your VPC is important.

A well-crafted VPC diagram can:

- Provide a clear, high-level overview of your network architecture.

- Help communicate your design to stakeholders and team members.

- Serve as a reference for implementation and troubleshooting.

- Help you spot potential issues or areas for optimization in your design.

Enterprise, Network, and Cybersecurity Architects play a critical role in defining the strategic vision of an organization's technology landscape. They frequently use architecture diagrams to communicate complex systems, security models, and infrastructure designs in a clear and structured way.

These visual representations serve as a blueprint for implementation, guiding engineers and technical teams in deploying, configuring, and maintaining the systems that support core business functions and capabilities.

Architects ensure that IT solutions align with business goals, compliance requirements, and security best practices by translating high-level objectives into actionable technical plans. In short, a diagram is a powerful tool for turning your VPC plan into a tangible, communicable artifact.

## Using draw.io

For this exercise, we'll be using [draw.io](https://draw.io). Draw.io has a wide variety of shapes and icons perfect for illustrating cloud architectures.

The shape library on the left is where you can find VPC components like subnets, gateways, and instances.

You may want to add the AWS specific pack to draw your VPC. Instructions for doing this can be found below.

## Building your diagram

Now, let's start translating your VPC plan into a visual diagram.
Some tips as you build:

- Use the **AWS4** shape library for official AWS icons. You can find VPC-specific shapes under **AWS4** / **Group** / **VPC**.

- Use different colors or styles to differentiate between public and private subnets, or different application tiers.

- Use connectors to show the flow of traffic between components.

- Add labels to your shapes to indicate their function or key configurations.

- Keep your diagram clean and uncluttered. Only include the necessary level of detail.

Feel free to get creative with your layout and style!

The goal is to create a diagram that's clear, accurate, and easy to understand at a glance.

## Reviewing and refining

Once you have a draft of your VPC diagram, take a step back and review it with a critical eye.

Ask yourself:

- Is the overall layout clear and easy to follow? For example, is the chosen CIDR block clearly shown?

- Are all the necessary components and connections included?

- Are the labels and styling consistent and informative?

- Does the diagram accurately reflect my VPC plan?

If anything seems unclear or out of place, don't hesitate to refine your diagram. Iteration is a natural part of the design process.

Once you're satisfied with your diagram, save it and export it as a PDF to submit.
